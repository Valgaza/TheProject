# Lakeview Skin Clinic: Superficial Fungal Infection Protocol

Version 3, approved September 2026. A fictional document written for the Nexus
demonstration; Lakeview Skin Clinic and everyone named here are invented.

## Scope

This protocol covers superficial dermatophyte infections of the skin (tinea
corporis, tinea cruris and tinea pedis) in adults seen at Lakeview Skin Clinic.
Nail infections (onychomycosis) and scalp infections (tinea capitis) are out of
scope and follow the separate nail and scalp protocol.

## Confirming the diagnosis

Confirm the diagnosis with KOH microscopy of a skin scraping before starting
any oral antifungal. Send a fungal culture as well when the infection is
recurrent, extensive, or has not responded to a previous course of treatment.

## First-line treatment: localised infection

For a localised infection, the first-line treatment is a topical antifungal:

- terbinafine 1% cream once daily for 2 weeks, or
- clotrimazole 1% cream twice daily for 4 weeks.

Continue the cream for 1 week after the skin looks clear. Do not use combination
creams that contain a topical steroid (for example clotrimazole with
betamethasone): the steroid lets the infection spread.

## Second-line treatment: extensive or failed topical treatment

Move to oral treatment when the infection is extensive, or when 4 weeks of
correct topical treatment has not worked.

- Oral terbinafine 250 mg once daily for 2 to 4 weeks is the second-line
  treatment.
- Check baseline liver function tests (LFTs) before starting oral terbinafine.
  Do not start oral terbinafine in a patient with active liver disease or
  abnormal LFTs.
- Oral itraconazole 100 mg twice daily for 2 weeks is the alternative when
  oral terbinafine is unsuitable or has not worked. Avoid itraconazole in heart
  failure, and check the patient's other medicines for interactions first.

## Suspected terbinafine resistance

Suspect resistance when there is no improvement after 4 weeks of oral
terbinafine taken as prescribed. Then:

1. send a fungal culture with sensitivity testing;
2. switch to oral itraconazole 200 mg once daily;
3. refer the patient to Dr. Farhan Iyer, consultant dermatologist.

## Follow-up

Review patients on oral treatment 2 weeks after starting. Stop treatment when
the skin is clinically clear, and tell the patient to come back if it returns
within 3 months.

## Advice for patients

Keep the area clean and dry, wash towels and clothing at 60 °C, and treat
household members who have the same rash at the same time.
